    Writing¶

    The words on a screen are part of the design. This chapter says how they should read. The short version: plain, friendly, short, and about the person, not about the system.

    Voice¶

    • Plain. Use the word a friend would use. "Leave room", not "Terminate session".
    • Friendly. Warm, calm and direct. Never cute, never stern.
    • Short. One idea per line. Cut any word that does not help.
    • About the person. Talk to them ("You can invite people here"), not about the system ("The invite functionality is available").
    • No blame. Say what happened, not what they did wrong. "That name is taken", not "You entered an invalid name".
    • No exclamation marks. Not even for good news.
    • No emoji in interface copy. Not in labels, messages or empty states. (People may type emoji in messages. That is their words, not ours.)

    Sentence case everywhere¶

    Capitalise the first word and proper names, and nothing else. This applies to headings, buttons, tabs, menu items, table headers, tags and tooltips.

    • "Create channel", not "Create Channel".
    • "Voice rooms", not "VOICE ROOMS" and not "Voice Rooms".

    Never use uppercase styling to make a label look like a heading. Use a text style (see Tokens and colour).

    Button labels are verbs¶

    A button says what will happen, in a verb and, where it helps, the thing it acts on.

    • "Leave room", "Delete channel", "Send invite", "Save changes".
    • Never "OK", "Yes", "No", "Submit" or "Confirm" on their own. They do not say what will happen.
    • The safe choice in a pair is "Cancel". The other one is the verb.
    • A busy button keeps its label. Do not swap it for "Loading...". The button already shows that it is busy.

    Confirm a destructive action by naming what is lost¶

    A dialog that asks for a destructive action names the thing and what goes with it, in the title or the description.

    • Title: "Delete #general?"
    • Description: "This removes 214 messages for everyone. You cannot undo it."
    • Buttons: "Cancel" and "Delete channel".

    A bare "Are you sure?" is not enough. See Dialog in Components for where the safe action goes.

    Errors say what happened, then what to do¶

    Write the error in two parts: what happened, and what the person can do next. Use the same shape every time: "Couldn't load channels. Try again."

    • Start with the fact. "Couldn't upload photo.", "That invite has expired."
    • End with a next step, when there is one. "Try again.", "Ask for a new one." When a button already offers the step, the words leave it out: an error with a Try again button reads "Couldn't load members", not "Couldn't load members. Try again." above "Try again".
    • Do not show codes, stack traces or the word "error" on its own. Do not say "Something went wrong" when you know what did.
    • Do not blame: "That file is too big. The limit is 25 MB.", not "You chose a file that is too big."

    Empty states say what will appear and how to start¶

    An empty list says what will show up here, and gives the first step.

    • "No voice rooms yet. Create one to talk with your community."
    • Put a button under it when there is an action: "Create voice room".
    • Do not leave a blank panel, and do not write "Nothing here".

    Counts, plurals and numbers¶

    • Write the number and the right word: "1 member", "2 members", "0 members". Never "member(s)".
    • Use numerals, not words: "3 unread", not "three unread".
    • Above 99, a count badge shows "99+".
    • Use a thousands separator in text: "1,204 messages".
    • Sizes keep a space before the unit: "25 MB".

    Times¶

    • Show the time of day in the person's own format, such as "8:24 PM" or "20:24". Do not force one.
    • Recent things read as "just now", "5 minutes ago", "2h ago". Older ones show the date.
    • Say "Seen 2h ago", not "Last seen: 2 hours ago".
    • Never show a time zone name unless the person is comparing zones.

    Accessible names¶

    Every control has a name that a screen reader speaks. A control that has only an icon gets its name from its label. A control that shows a picture and a state puts the facts in the name, in this order, joined by commas:

    name, state, count

    • Avatar: "Ana Ruiz, away".
    • DockItem: "Couch Co-op, current, 3 unread, reconnecting". Each part is there only when it applies.

    The name is plain words, in sentence case, with no emoji and no symbols. Do not repeat the role in the name: the page already says it is a button, so write "Mute", not "Mute button". See Avatar and DockItem in Components.

    Before and after¶

    Where Before After Why
    Confirm a deletion "Are you sure?" with OK and Cancel "Delete #general?" with "Cancel" and "Delete channel" Names what is lost and what the button does
    Leaving a voice room "Disconnect" "Leave room" Plain verb, the word people use
    Loading channels failed "Error 500" "Couldn't load channels. Try again." What happened, then what to do
    A load error with a Try again button "Couldn't load members", "Try again.", then the button "Try again" "Couldn't load members", then the button "Try again" The button is the next step; do not say it twice
    Empty voice rooms list "Nothing here" "No voice rooms yet. Create one to talk with your community." Says what will appear and how to start
    Invite expired "Invalid invite code" "That invite has expired. Ask for a new one." Says what happened, with a next step, no blame
    Upload too large "You selected a file that is too large" "That file is too big. The limit is 25 MB." No blame, gives the limit
    Button after an upload "Submit" "Save photo" A verb that says what will happen
    Section heading "VOICE ROOMS" "Voice rooms" Sentence case, no uppercase labels
    Member count "1 member(s)" "1 member" The right plural
    Community invite sent "Invite sent successfully!" "Invite sent" No exclamation mark, no filler
    Upload in progress Button text "Loading..." Button "Upload file", busy The label stays, the button shows it is busy
    Unread count on a community "Couch Co-op (3)" "Couch Co-op, 3 unread" A reader hears what the number means
