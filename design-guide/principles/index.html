    Principles¶

    These are the ideas that make a screen look and feel like Semaphore. They are short on purpose. When you are unsure how something should look, go back to them before you reach for a new colour, a new shape or a new component.

    Each principle names the rule or component that carries it, and one live story you can open to see it. A story id is the file name plus the export name in lower-case words joined by hyphens (see Components).

    1. Everything sits in islands on a ground¶

    The ground is the page. Islands are large rounded panels that float on it. A screen is a few islands and nothing outside them. Inside an island go rows, cards, text and controls. Only an overlay (a dialog, a sheet, a menu) floats above the islands.

    Carried by: Island and Card in Components, and the layer table in "How a screen is built". The gap between islands belongs to the layout, not to the island.

    Story: kit-island--layout.

    2. One accent, and it means two things¶

    The accent colour is for "this one is selected" and "you are here". It is never decoration. There are exactly two signs:

    • The solid accent pill on a list row: the selected row, such as the channel you are in.
    • The accent outline on a card: you are in this, such as a voice room you joined, or it is the current item.

    Do not swap them, and do not use the accent for anything else: not for a heading, a divider, a badge that only looks nice, or a background. The one other place the accent appears is the primary button, which is the one main action of a surface.

    Carried by: "Two signs for this one" under Card in Components, and the accent, onAccent and accentText roles in Tokens and colour.

    Story: kit-card--voice-rooms (the outline), kit-list-row--states (the pill).

    3. Soft, round shapes¶

    Corners are round and the shapes are calm. Every corner comes from the radius tokens: islands 24, cards 18, fields 14, small controls 6 and 10, and fully round for buttons, pills and tags. You do not pick a radius. The component has already chosen it.

    Carried by: the radius scale in Tokens and colour.

    Story: kit-button--variants.

    4. Compact message rows, not bubbles¶

    A message is a compact row: the person's picture, their name and the time on one line, and the words under it, with no bubble around them. Rows keep a long conversation short and easy to scan. Bubbles are not part of the system. If a second style is ever offered, it will be an appearance option that the owner decides.

    Carried by: the text styles name, body and meta, and the Avatar sizes, in Tokens and colour, and the kit's MessageRow (see Components).

    Story: kit-message-row--states.

    5. Colour comes from roles, and roles come from one base colour¶

    The person using the app chooses one base colour, a mode (dark or light) and an intensity. The app derives the whole palette from those three. So a screen never names a colour. It names a role: surface, text, accentText, danger. The same screen then works in every palette the person can pick.

    The one exception is data: a person's or community's own colour, shown by Avatar.

    Carried by: "The 16 roles" in Tokens and colour.

    Story: kit-text--tones-tinted shows the roles in a tinted palette.

    6. Readable by construction, and never by colour alone¶

    The palette promises contrast. Text roles reach at least 4.5 to 1 on the layers they may sit on, and the palette is built so that the promise holds for any base colour. You keep the promise by using the roles where the table says they may sit, for example textDim never on a fill.

    Colour is never the only sign. Presence has a shape as well as a colour (a filled dot, a dot with a bar, a hollow ring). A selected row has a pill. A danger action has a label that says what it does. Anyone who cannot tell the colours apart still gets the meaning.

    Carried by: "What the palette promises" in Tokens and colour, and the presence rules under Avatar in Components.

    Story: kit-avatar--presence.

    7. Controls behave the same everywhere¶

    Every control has a label, even when the label is hidden from the eye. A busy control (loading) stays focusable and ignores clicks; only disabled takes a control out of the tab order. Hover shows only on devices that hover, so a touch screen never has a stuck hover. Pressed and keyboard focus always show.

    Carried by: "Rules every component follows" in Components and "Loading is not disabled" in the README.

    Story: kit-button--states.

    8. Motion is small and optional¶

    Things fade or move a little, using the timing tokens (120, 200 and 320 milliseconds). Nothing bounces, spins for show or draws the eye away from the words. When a person asks their system for reduced motion, the motion stops and the meaning stays: a busy bar becomes a full, still bar.

    Carried by: the timings in Tokens and colour and the motion rule in Components.

    Story: kit-linear-progress--values.

    9. Sentence case and plain words¶

    Every label, heading and button is in sentence case: "Create channel", not "Create Channel" and never "CREATE CHANNEL". Words are plain and friendly. How to write them is in Writing.

    Carried by: the text styles in Tokens and colour, none of which is uppercase, and Writing.

    Story: kit-dialog--confirm.

    10. The web and the native client show the same thing¶

    Both clients use the same tokens, the same palette and the same kit names and options. A screen built from the kit looks and behaves the same in both. The two are kept in step by generated files, not by hand. A change to a system piece is made in both clients.

    Carried by: the generated tokens and palette vectors described in design/README.md, and the paired kits. See Extending for how a change lands in both.

    Story: kit-dock--communities.

    What we never do¶

    • Borders on islands. An island has no border. A shadow lifts it. Only an overlay has a thin border.
    • Blur, filters, blend modes or inset shadows. The native client cannot draw them. Flat fills, plain shadows and simple shapes only.
    • Raw colours or pixel numbers on a screen. No #rrggbb, no 13px. Pick the role or the token by name.
    • Uppercase labels. Not for headings, not for tags, not for buttons.
    • A second layout. A screen uses one of the layout templates. It does not invent its own. A different look is a different skin (a set of token values), not a different layout.
    • Old and new looks mixed on one screen. A person sees the old app or the new one. A converted screen never holds an old-style piece.
    • A one-off component for a single screen. If the kit does not have what you need, add it to the system (see Extending). Do not build it into the screen.
